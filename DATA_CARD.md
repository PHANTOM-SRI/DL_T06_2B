# Data Card – Romanized Telugu–English Film Review Sentiment (SYNTHETIC)

**Team 6 · MGIT Deep Learning · Task 2B**

## WARNING
This dataset is **synthetic**. Reviews were generated by a clause-template grammar and are **not** real user reviews. Labels were assigned by the generation rule, **not by human annotators**. Do not use it to estimate real-world accuracy.

## Source and collection
- Generated in the notebook (Section 2) with fixed seed 42; fictional film titles only.
- Language: Romanized Telugu (Latin script) mixed with English. Telugu-script reviews: 5985.
- Annotation guide: `ANNOTATION_GUIDE.md`. No human annotation; no inter-annotator agreement.

## Size and class distribution
- Total labelled reviews: **6000** (Positive 2000, Negative 2000, Mixed 2000). Classes are balanced by construction.
- Missing values: 0. Duplicate review texts: 0.
- Tokens per review: mean 31.4, median 17, max 131.

## Unseen-data challenge sets
`unseen_validation_SYNTHETIC.csv` (600) and `unseen_test_SYNTHETIC.csv` (900): every review has at least one token absent from the training vocabulary (novel spellings, slang/English words, aspects). Validation and test use different halves of the novel lexicon. **Labels are rule-assigned and the slang lexicon was not reviewed by a native Telugu speaker.** Flags `unseen_spelling` / `unseen_word` mean: the unseen token was / was not repaired by the 0.8-similarity spelling normaliser, so `unseen_word` also covers heavily altered spellings.

## Benchmark pool
`cpu_benchmark_pool_10000_SYNTHETIC.csv`: 10,000 distinct generated reviews, disjoint from the labelled corpus, used only for CPU timing.

## Known limitations
- Closed vocabulary of roughly 170 distinct tokens; models can memorise phrases, so high scores are expected and uninformative about real data.
- Clause templates are shared across groups, so group-aware splitting reduces but does not eliminate template overlap.
- Spelling variation is injected from a small table, far narrower than real Romanized Telugu usage.
- Balanced classes, short grammatical structure and fictional titles do not match real reviews.
- Sarcasm and hedged-ambiguity are template-defined, not naturally occurring.
